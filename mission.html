Our mission

Help farmers make the switch — and get through the dip

Farming covers about 70% of the UK's land. Farmed regeneratively, that land can rebuild soil, hold back floods, bring wildlife home and pull carbon out of the air — while still growing good food. Plenty of farmers want to make that change. Too many can't afford the years it takes.

The problem: the dip

Moving a farm from intensive to regenerative isn't a switch you flip. Fertiliser and sprays are cut before the soil has recovered enough to replace them. Yields fall. New kit, new livestock and new skills cost money up front. For a few years, income drops — and it's those years, not the destination, that stop farmers from starting.

There's already good support for the practice: peer networks, advisers, demonstration farms, outcome payments and public schemes. What almost nobody offers is money to live on through the middle of the change.

What we'll do

  1. Bridge the income dip

    Multi-year grants sized to each farm's own transition plan, tapering off as the new system starts to pay its way.

  2. Back the farmer, not just the practice

    Pair the money with the networks, mentors and advisers already doing this work well, so no one makes the change alone.

  3. Measure what changes

    Track soil, water, wildlife, carbon and farm income with the measurement tools farmers already use, and publish what we learn.

  4. Hand on, not hang on

    Plan every grant's end from day one: a hand-off to market premiums, outcome payments or public schemes once the farm stands on its own.

How we'll work

  • Partner, don't duplicate. Fill the gap others leave, and send farmers to the people who already do the rest best.
  • Farmer-led. Plans are written by the farmers who have to live with them.
  • Fair and open. Clear rules on who we fund, why, and what we ask in return.
  • Honest about the hard questions. How to define the dip fairly, and how to avoid paying for change that would have happened anyway.
